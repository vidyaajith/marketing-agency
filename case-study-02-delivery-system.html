The Challenge

Same stages, nine different experiences.

Lead, Signed, Kickoff, Strategy, Production, Review, Launch, Reporting, Renewal. Every client moved through these. No two moved through them the same way. Onboarding was improvised, approvals slipped, launches were fire drills, and QA didn't formally exist.

Image Placeholder The before state: inconsistent PM handoffs across the client lifecycle img-01-before-state.png · 16:9 · 1600×900

Underneath it, four handoff gaps

  • Improvised onboarding. No standard path from signed contract to kickoff.
  • Approvals that slipped. The review stage had no defined gate or owner.
  • Launches as fire drills. Nothing was checked before work went live.
  • No formal QA. Nothing required a second set of eyes.

My Approach

Standardize the handoffs.

The variance wasn't in the nine stages, every client moved through the same ones. It was in the handoffs between them. That's where the work focused.

01

Onboarding workflow

Signed contract to kickoff call, the same path every time.

02

SOP library

Every recurring delivery function documented, so no step depends on memory.

03

Launch checklist

Applied to every account before anything goes live.

04

QA framework

Defined approval gates. Nothing ships without a second set of eyes.

The Leak

Delivery quality depended on which PM's memory the process lived in, not on the process itself.

The Build

Four mechanisms that make it hold.

01

Communication Cadence

Clients stop asking because they already know.

Set a standing cadence so clients always know where things stand without chasing a PM for an update.

Status stopped being something a client had to request
02

Client Health Scoring

Risk flagged before it becomes a complaint.

Built a scoring system that flags at-risk accounts from behavior and delivery signals, so problems surface as data instead of as an angry email.

At-risk accounts surface as a score, not a complaint
Image Placeholder The health scorecard, populated img-02-health-scorecard.png · 16:9 · 1600×900
03

Escalation Rules

Every PM knows exactly when to raise a hand, and to whom.

Defined the specific triggers that require escalation and the specific person they go to, so issues stop dying quietly with whichever PM happened to notice first.

  • Named triggers. Specific conditions that require escalation, not judgment calls.
  • Named owner. Every trigger routes to a specific person, not "whoever's around."
Image Placeholder The escalation matrix or RACI img-03-escalation-matrix.png · 16:9 · 1600×900
04

Renewal Workflow

Renewal stopped being a scramble.

Built renewal into the lifecycle itself as a defined stage with its own triggers and owner, instead of a fire drill that started the week a contract was about to lapse.

Lifecycle stages now have a defined owner and trigger, including renewal.
