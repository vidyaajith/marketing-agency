The Challenge

Nobody saw the same picture.

Multiple PMs, multiple service lines, strong delivery talent. What was missing wasn't skill, it was the connective tissue between the work. Every PM ran a different system and a personal method for tracking it.

Image Placeholder The before state: individual PM trackers and disconnected reports img-01-before-state.png · 16:9 · 1600×900

Underneath it, four operational gaps

  • No shared system. Every PM working from a different tool and personal method.
  • Blind decision-making. Leadership deciding from incomplete information.
  • Founder as escalation point. Pulled into delivery conversations daily.
  • Reactive signal. Client issues surfacing as complaints, not early warnings.

My Approach

Diagnosis before tooling.

No tool was touched for the first [X] weeks. Every system in use was mapped, every team member interviewed individually, and what leadership believed was happening was compared to what was actually happening.

01

System mapping

Catalogued every tool, spreadsheet, and personal tracker in active use across the team.

02

Individual interviews

Talked to each team member alone, away from how the workflow was described from the top.

03

Belief vs. reality

Compared leadership's mental model of account status against what was actually happening on the ground.

04

Gap analysis

The space between those two pictures became the real scope of the project.

The Leak

Quality depended on which PM owned the account, so growth multiplied risk instead of revenue.

The Build

Four moves, one operating system.

01

Executive Dashboard

One live view, not four different opinions.

Built a real-time status view across every active account, pulling from wherever the work actually lived, so leadership could see the business instead of asking about it.

One panel, replacing four PM opinions
Image Placeholder The live executive dashboard img-02-executive-dashboard.png · 16:9 · 1600×900
02

Department Scorecards

KPIs owned by the people who move them.

Each function got its own scorecard, tracked against metrics that function's lead was directly responsible for moving, replacing vague status updates with numbers anyone could check.

  • Owned by function leads. No department relies on the founder to interpret its own numbers.
  • Same metrics, every week. Comparable over time instead of reconstructed from memory.
Image Placeholder A department scorecard, populated img-03-scorecard.png · 16:9 · 1600×900
03

Automated Weekly Report

Zero manual input, every week.

A leadership report now generates itself from the same live data, arriving on schedule with no one compiling it by hand.

04

Reporting Conventions

Information pushes up. The founder stopped pulling it down.

Set conventions for how and when information moves from PM to leadership, so status updates became a built-in habit of the system rather than something the founder had to chase.
